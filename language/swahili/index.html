      Frequently asked questions

      What is the best AI for Swahili?

      Gemini 3.1 Pro (direct audio) was the highest-scoring chain in our 2026-08-30 Swahili benchmark. It achieved a Swahili Index of 95/100 with a median response time of 5.8 seconds across 24 questions; see the benchmark results. This result applies to the tested questions and workflows, not every use case.

      Can AI models understand and answer questions in Swahili?

      Yes—the tested workflows processed spoken Swahili questions and produced answers in English. Across 19 chains, the Swahili Index ranged from 65 to 95/100 in the benchmark results. These are complete-workflow results, not standalone speech-recognition scores or a test of answers written in Swahili.

      Can I run Swahili AI on my own servers?

      Open-weight language models such as DeepSeek V4 and Kimi K3 offer a starting point for running Swahili AI on your own servers. A fully local voice workflow also requires speech recognition that supports Swahili on local hardware. Choose hardware that meets the models’ requirements and check each component’s licence and language support before deployment.

      Which Swahili AI models are best for phone calls and WhatsApp?

      For phone-call pilots, Gemini 3.1 Pro (direct audio) is the highest-scoring chain below our 10-second median response-time target (95/100, 5.8 seconds). For WhatsApp voice-note replies, Gemini 3.1 Pro (direct audio) had the highest Swahili Index (95/100) in the benchmark results. These timings exclude a complete phone connection and spoken reply; test telephony, network and text-to-speech latency before launch.

      What does the Swahili Index measure?

      The Swahili Index is the mean semantic-similarity score between a workflow’s answers and the reference answers, scaled from 0 to 100. The evaluation CSV records scores for the 24 benchmark questions, with a higher Index indicating closer agreement in meaning. It is not the percentage of questions answered correctly, a standalone transcription score or a safety certification.

      How were the Swahili AI models evaluated?

      On 2026-08-30, we evaluated 19 chains using 24 spoken Swahili questions: general-knowledge questions about Kenya. The workflows used direct audio or speech recognition with a language model, and their answers were scored by semantic similarity against the reference answers supplied in the dataset; the evaluation CSV contains the questions, references, scores and run links. Scores are averaged and response times are medians; this small dataset does not establish performance across all topics, accents or real-world conditions. These benchmark results are not a clinical safety assessment. Any clinical or patient-facing use requires qualified clinical review.

      What is the environmental impact of Swahili AI models?

      Our EcoCost estimates cover text-model inference for 15 of the 19 tested Swahili chains, not their full environmental impact. The EcoCost snapshot reports estimated CO₂e per 1,000 questions and uncertainty ranges with low confidence. Speech recognition and direct-audio inference are excluded, and missing estimates are not zero; these data cannot establish the lowest-impact complete voice workflow.
